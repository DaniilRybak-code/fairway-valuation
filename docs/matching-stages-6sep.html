How Fairway picks a founder's comparables

Every founder is matched twice: once against listed companies, once against private funding rounds. The two never mix. A comparable has to survive every stage below, in this order, and the page can say at which stage every name got in or was kept out.

The profile

Ten tags describe the founder: what kind of business (archetype, and a second one), which end market, who buys, how it is sold, how it charges, and the product words from their website. No revenue, no size, no date is used to choose a comparable. Size and recency may only trim and order.

The lender fence

A lender is priced on its book, not its revenue, and a lender's multiple carries its funding and credit losses. So lenders and deposit banks only ever price other lenders, and nothing else may price them. Rule A10.

The growth band

Mature and hyper-growth rounds do not belong in one comparison. Bands are fitted on private rounds themselves: mature below 65 per cent a year, growing 65 to 162, hyper above. Neighbouring bands may sit together; opposite ends may not. An unknown band on either side never excludes anything.

4
The score

Every survivor is scored against the founder. Shared product words weigh most (up to 12 points: 3 for a whole tag in common, less for a shared word, and a word carried by many companies is worth very little). Then the archetype, the end market, the function, the buyer, the revenue model, how it is sold. The score ranks; on its own it never admits a name.

5
The relevance gate

Nothing with nothing to do with the founder. A candidate must share product vocabulary (any shared word at all) or the same specific end market. Sharing only the archetype label is not evidence, because the labels are too coarse: Vertical Software holds stock exchanges and restaurant software. Rule A12.

6
The tier ladder

Each survivor is labelled by how close it is. DIRECT: anchored on real product words (3 points or more), or the same specific end market plus the same archetype. ADJACENT: does the same kind of thing (shares an archetype) but the products differ. BROAD: same family only; shown as context, never prices. The set fills DIRECT first, then ADJACENT; it falls to BROAD only when both are empty. A mixed set is labelled by its weakest member.

Core also needs axis B, who it sells to: a peer in the same end market, a horizontal peer, the same primary archetype, or 3 points of product words. Names that qualify but miss axis B go to the wider ring, never lost.
Same ladder. Rows whose revenue is a range rather than a figure are kept only on 3 points of product words.
7
The floors

Three of them. Absolute: below 5.0 points a name is not a comparable. Relative: below 45 per cent of the best score in the tier it is dropped, so one excellent match raises the bar for everyone. Adequacy: a DIRECT set needs its best name above 12.0, because 45 per cent of a bad score is a worse score.

One round per company

One company, one vote, never an average of its rounds. Take the latest round, unless the priced rounds are more than 30 per cent apart; then the company was a different business at each one, and the round whose revenue is closest to the founder's is taken. A round that cannot price this founder loses the tie to one that can.

Order and trim

DIRECT names first, then most recent first within a tier; a direct name is never displaced by a newer adjacent one. Three to five names on an almost perfect match, five to seven where the match is weaker, because a weak set needs the spread to do the work.

10
The top-up

A lane that cannot price is not a lane. If too few of the chosen names carry a usable multiple on the founder's basis, the lane reaches down its own ranked list for names that do, giving up the weakest name that cannot. Topped-up names are marked so the page can say they were reached for.

11
The rescue, recorded

If a lane still cannot price two names, both lanes are rebuilt once with the archetype label allowed as evidence. The rescue is kept only if it adds a priced name, and every name it admits is printed on the No-comps list. A founder served on a label is a hole in the database, not a feature. Rule A12.

The ranges, one per reading

Each lane's names are priced on each reading the founder's quiz fork supports, and readings never mix: net revenue with net, gross with gross, book with book, a count with the same kind of count. One priced name draws as a point, never a range. A spread wider than 6x draws as a scatter, not a band. Names held out of the medians stay visible and do not price. Listed and private are never averaged together.

Revenue (or book and earnings for a lender). Listed rows carry no gross or net label, so a gross range is not offered here.

What the gate scores, and what today's engine says

A test company passes when it has one public lane and one private lane that each price at least two names, three distinct names in all, and at least one lane resting on more than a shared word. The No-comps list holds two kinds of entry: founders served on a label by stage 11, and founders served nothing at all. It is resolved once, in bulk, after the 18 September march.

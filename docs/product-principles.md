# Fairway product principles

**These are not notes. They are the rules the product is judged against.** Read this before designing anything on the result screen, and before writing a line of the valuation engine.

## 1. The lesson from the competitive scan

Every competitor found in the August 2026 scan is one of two things. A long mandatory form whose "report" is a brochure, or a calculator that prints a number with a pre-fixed multiple and no sourcing. **Not one of them tells you where a single number came from.**

That is not a gap in their product. It is the whole category.

So the hover-source behaviour is not a nice detail on our football field, **it is the product**, and the landing page should say so in plain words rather than leaving the visitor to discover it.

## 2. What follows from that, without exception

**A number on the screen without a reference metric beside it is a competitor's product.** Every row of the football field carries three things: the parameter, the reference metrics that went into it, and the implied value. Drop the middle column and we have shipped a calculator with bars on it. This has been got wrong once already; it is the single easiest way to destroy the reason this business exists.

**Every figure is either sourced or locked.** There is no third state. If we cannot attribute a number to a named source with a vintage, we do not draw it. Locked is honest. Plausible is not.

**A judgement is shown as a judgement.** The illiquidity discount is our opinion, so it is labelled as our opinion, shown as a range, and shown at all. Burying a 30% haircut inside a number is what the calculators do.

**A locked row that is locked because the data is not built yet must never be dressed as a paywall.** It says "in build". Founders forgive an unfinished row. They do not forgive being charged for one that was never coming.

**Never convert, adjust or blend at a rate we cannot point at.** Currency conversion waited until there was an ECB reference rate with a date on it. Before that the row simply did not plot, and the page said why.

**The last round is a marker, never an input.** A field that lets the last price set the next price is not a field, it is an echo, and it reproduces whatever mispricing the founder is trying to escape.

**Say the uncomfortable thing.** If the range implies a flat or down round, the page says so. If the two strongest methods are not built yet, the page says so. Credibility is the entire moat and it is spent, not earned, every time we let an impression stand that we know is generous.

## 3. The test to apply before shipping any change to the result screen

Point at any number on the page and ask: **can the founder find out where this came from, in one tap, without asking us?**

If the answer is no, it is not finished, however good it looks.

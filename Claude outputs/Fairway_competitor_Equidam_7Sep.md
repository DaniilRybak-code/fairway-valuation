# EQUIDAM: the one direct competitor, read properly

**Written 7 September 2026, 19:45 UK (Fable), on Daniil's instruction of the same evening: "Equidam is potentially an interesting one, and it might be a direct competitor. We need to be looking at that... we need to keep an eye on it, and we need to look into it more deeply."**
**Every figure comes from a page read on 7 September 2026; the source and URL sit beside it or in the Sources at the foot. Lines marked INFERENCE are judgement.**

---

## In five lines

1. Equidam is a thirteen-year-old, bootstrapped, six-person Dutch company that has valued about 160,000 companies and sells about 2,000 paid valuations a year, roughly 70 per cent to founders and 30 per cent to investors. It is real, profitable and slow-moving.
2. Its number comes from five textbook methods blended into one range, fed mostly by the founder's own three-year projections and by industry-average multiples from a 30,000-company public database. That is the "one range, methodology not explained" Daniil saw.
3. Since December 2025 a founder can pick named public comparables from Equidam's weekly list and type in private-round data themselves. So Equidam now has a comparables feature, but the founder has to know the comparables. Fairway's engine finds them. That is the difference to defend.
4. Equidam already sells to investors ("Screening Deal Flow", "Portfolio valuation", a professional licence and an API). The investor read Fairway may build in Horizon 2 is a market Equidam is already in, at small scale.
5. Nothing to change before the pilot. Two things to keep: the landing copy that sells peer selection (that is precisely what Equidam does not do), and a quarterly look at Equidam's product updates page for the two moves that would narrow the gap.

## What Equidam is

- Founded 2013 in the Netherlands by Daniel Faloppa; "lean 6-person team based in the Netherlands and Spain"; profitable, no outside funding mentioned; "roughly 70% founders and 30% investors"; about 2,000 startups a year (Built in Europe newsletter, 11 May 2025, a secondary source based on an interview).
- Headline claims on its own site: 160,000 companies valued since 2013; 90 countries and 641 industries; $5bn raised by users; "94% positive investor feedback (133 survey responses)"; logos of Y Combinator, Techstars, 500 Global and others (equidam.com, read 7 Sep 2026).
- Sold to four groups: startups (fundraising, M&A, equity compensation), investors (portfolio valuation, deal screening), advisors (client valuations and lead generation), platforms (API).

## How the product works

- The founder answers "10 questions for an initial, AI-powered, valuation estimate", then "37 multiple choice questions that capture team, business model, milestones, IP, competitive landscape and more", then enters three years of financial projections. Advanced users can "adjust the full range of valuation parameters, including discount rate, EBITDA multiples, survival rates, WACC, and more" (How it works page).
- Five methods, blended: Scorecard and Checklist (qualitative, for pre-revenue companies), DCF with long-term growth, DCF with multiples, and the VC method. Weights are "adaptable" but the defaults by stage are not published (Methodology page).
- Data behind the multiples: a "database of 30.000+ public companies" with revenue and EBITDA multiples updated weekly, plus "industry averages" tailored to "90+ countries and 600+ industries" (Methodology page). Benchmarks ("Valuation Delta") compare the founder to "over 170.000 peers" from "Equidam + Crunchbase data" by stage, industry and country; the page does not say whether individual rounds are named (Benchmarks page).
- The Equidam AI "gathers your company's public data and drafts projections and valuation"; the AI drafts the inputs, the methods produce the number (home page).
- The report: 21 to 34 pages, PDF, Excel or Markdown, on the paid tiers.

## The December 2025 change, which is the one that matters to Fairway

Post of 18 December 2025, "Sensitivity Analysis and Enhanced Multiples". Three ways to set the exit multiple now exist: the industry default (Equidam's computed multiple from public company data); "Specific Comparables", where the founder can "select specific companies that genuinely resemble your business" from "a weekly-updated list of multiples from all public companies worldwide" and "add private company data" ("If you know that a competitor raised at a specific multiple, or that a comparable company was acquired at a known valuation, you can incorporate that information"); and a direct input with a written justification that "appears in the valuation report". Two sensitivity tables were added (growth against margin; discount rate against exit multiple).

INFERENCE: this is Equidam admitting that industry averages do not persuade investors, and handing the comparables problem to the founder. Fairway's whole engine exists to solve that problem for the founder: the archetype, the product vocabulary, the end market, the relevance gate, the private rounds with basis and period discipline, the sources on every number. Equidam gives the founder a list of 30,000 tickers and a text box.

A second signal, 9 May 2025: Equidam added a "Company Category" classification (supply side: how the company is built and delivers value), adapted from Y Combinator's categories, alongside its TRBC industry. Its own words: "For now, this update serves a data collection purpose only. It does not affect valuations." INFERENCE: that is the first step towards business-model matching, which is Fairway's core idea. It is a data-collection step today, with "long-term potential methodology integration" named as the plan.

## Pricing (read 7 Sep 2026)

- Essential: free, "full 5-method valuation engine", the range on screen, multiple scenarios, "no downloadable report", "no benchmarks".
- Advanced: $412.25 for a two-month access window inside a twelve-month cycle; exports; "Valuation Delta benchmarks (Equidam + Crunchbase)"; recent funding rounds and revenue and EBITDA benchmarks; sensitivity analysis.
- Expert: $1,063.25, same window; "Expert reviews your inputs and assumptions", a written findings summary and a "1:1 call to walk through the report".
- 409A: $1,990 a year (US option plans).
- Professional licence and API: custom pricing, for investors, advisors and platforms.

Beside Fairway's tiers (free reveal; $250 full field; $750 banker-reviewed presentation): Fairway is cheaper at every step. Equidam's Expert tier is the direct analogue of the $750 review and costs $1,063. INFERENCE: the $250 tier is priced well below the market's one comparable product; whether that is right is Daniil's call, and the pilot's conversion figures are the evidence to decide it on. Not a change for the launch.

## Where Equidam locks and where Fairway locks

Equidam's free tier shows the whole blended range and locks the document (no report, no benchmarks). Fairway's free tier shows the listed lane in full and locks the private-round figures (names visible, multiples blurred). INFERENCE: Equidam's lock says "you can see your number, you pay for something to send"; Fairway's says "you can see the public evidence, you pay for the private evidence and the banker". Both are defensible. One question for after the pilot, not a ruling now: what exactly a $250 founder can send to an investor, because at Equidam the sendable PDF is the reason to pay.

## What users say

- G2, 3.5 of 5 from two reviews (Nov 2023): "It is a platform that guides you through the startup valuation process step by step"; "small variables can make such a difference... and there is no way to validate if they are correct"; "It is so easy to have so much variability... if you change some key assumptions". Trustpilot could not be read (403).
- Equidam's own framing (19 Sep 2025, "Equidam vs AI valuation"): "Users see the math, the parameters, and the impact of each method on the final range"; it "connects valuation methods to maintained datasets (public comps, private round data, country risk inputs)"; LLM outputs risk "hallucination, stale context, or mismatched comparables".

INFERENCE: the complaint that survives in the reviews is exactly Daniil's: the number moves with assumptions nobody can check. A valuation built from named comparables with sources is the answer to that complaint. Equidam's own anti-LLM argument is also Fairway's argument, so Fairway should not sound like an "AI valuation"; it should sound like what it is, a comparables engine with a banker behind it.

## What to watch, and the two moves that would matter

Check quarterly (first check: early December 2026, after the pilot's numbers are in): the Equidam blog and product-update posts, its pricing page, and its investor pages.

The two moves that would narrow Fairway's gap, in order of danger:

1. **Engine-selected comparables.** If Equidam starts suggesting the specific comparables itself (the natural next step after "Specific Comparables" and the May 2025 categorisation), the difference becomes the quality of the peer selection rather than its existence. Fairway's answer is the machinery that landing page 3 already sells, and the No-comps list discipline.
2. **A private-round database shown to founders.** Today the founder types private data in. If Equidam ships a sourced private-round set with basis and period, Fairway's private lane loses its exclusivity. Fairway's answer is the sourcing discipline (every round on its URL) and the net/gross separation, which are hard to copy quickly.

Two moves that would not matter much: more methods (Fairway deliberately does not blend), and more industries (Fairway does not compete on breadth; the 6 Sep positioning rule stands).

## What to do about it now

Nothing that touches the launch. Three things to keep in mind:

- The landing copy is already aimed at the right difference: "every name has to earn its place" and "business model, not sector code" are the two lines Equidam cannot say. Keep them.
- Do not describe Fairway as an AI valuation. Equidam's best argument against LLM valuations is one Fairway agrees with; Fairway's number never comes from a model.
- After the pilot, look at three things with Equidam beside them: the $250 price, what a $250 founder can send to an investor, and whether the Horizon 2 investor read should be positioned against Equidam's screening line or somewhere Equidam is not (co-investor matching, base rates).
